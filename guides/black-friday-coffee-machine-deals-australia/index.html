Black Friday coffee machine deals in Australia: what to expect in 2026

Black Friday is 27 November 2026. Which coffee gear actually drops in price in Australia, when the real discounts land, and how to spot a fake RRP.

6 November 2026

Black Friday is Friday 27 November 2026. Cyber Monday is Monday 30 November. In Australia the sales no longer respect those dates — the discounting starts in the third week of November and runs through to the first week of December, with the deepest cuts usually landing somewhere in the middle.

This is the one time of year when home espresso gear genuinely gets cheap. It’s also the time of year when the fake discounts come out, and coffee machines are a favourite target because almost nobody knows what they normally cost.

Here’s what actually happens, based on what we’ve watched drop in previous years.

What genuinely discounts, and what doesn’t

Reliably discounted:

  • Mid-range Breville machines. The Barista Express, Bambino Plus and Barista Pro are the most heavily promoted home espresso machines in the country, and they’re cut hard. If you want one, this is the week.
  • Bean-to-cup machines from De’Longhi and Philips. These carry high RRPs and high margins, so there’s room to move. Discounts here can look dramatic — which is also why the RRPs are worth checking.
  • Nespresso machines and bundles. The machine is the loss leader; the pods are the business. Expect genuinely low machine prices, often bundled with a milk frother or a pod credit.
  • Accessories and consumables. Scales, tampers, jugs, cleaning tablets. Small discounts in dollar terms, but they stack, and these are the things that actually improve your coffee.

Rarely discounted much:

  • Entry-level machines already at a low price. There’s no margin left in a sub-$200 machine.
  • Enthusiast gear with controlled pricing. Niche grinders and boutique brands often have minimum advertised prices, so they move a little or not at all.
  • The current flagship. Whatever launched this year tends to hold its price; it’s last year’s model that gets cleared.

The inflated-RRP problem

The single most common trick is a “was” price nobody has paid for years.

A machine sits at a street price well below its official RRP all year. In November, the listing starts comparing against that RRP instead, and a routine price becomes “40% off” without the actual cost to you changing by a cent.

This is legal in Australia only up to a point — the ACCC treats a “was” price as misleading if the product hasn’t genuinely sold at that price recently — but enforcement is slow and the sale is over in a week.

The only defence is price history. Not the percentage on the badge, not the strikethrough. What the thing has actually sold for.

When to buy

The instinct is to wait for the Friday. That’s usually wrong.

  • Mid-November: early sales begin. Some of these are as good as anything that follows, particularly on accessories.
  • The week of the 23rd: the real discounting starts. Most of the year’s lowest prices on popular machines land in this window.
  • Friday 27th and the weekend: high traffic, heavy promotion. Prices are often the same as Tuesday’s, with more urgency attached.
  • Cyber Monday, 30 November: a second wave, sometimes on different stock. Worth a look if you missed out.
  • Early December: leftovers and clearance. Occasionally the best price of all on a discontinued model, but stock is thin and colours are limited.

Practical approach: decide what you want now, before the noise starts. Know what it has historically sold for. Then buy the moment it hits that number, rather than holding out for a mythical better price on the Friday. Popular machines sell out, and the discount doesn’t come back.

A short watchlist by budget

Under $400 — Breville Bambino Plus (needs a separate grinder), De’Longhi Dedica, Sunbeam Mini Barista.

$500 to $800 — Breville Barista Express is the default first “proper” machine for a reason: grinder built in, one box, done. The Barista Pro adds a faster heat-up and a better interface.

Bean-to-cup, press-a-button — De’Longhi Magnifica S, Philips 4400 Series, De’Longhi Rivelia Start. Worth it if nobody in the house wants a hobby.

Pods — Nespresso Vertuo Pop or a Vertuo Up bundle. Be honest about the ongoing pod cost before you commit.

Grinder first — if you already have a machine, a better grinder will do more for your coffee than a new machine will. The Smart Grinder Pro and Baratza Encore ESP are the usual recommendations.

The unglamorous advice

If your budget is tight, the best Black Friday purchase is often not a machine.

A scale with a timer, a tamper that fits your basket, and cleaning tablets will improve your coffee more than upgrading from a decent machine to a slightly better one. Together they cost less than a tank of petrol. We’ve written about the cheap upgrades that beat a new machine if you want the full argument.

Check your tamper size before you buy one: Breville machines use 54mm, most others use 58mm. It’s the most common mistake people make in a sale.

Before you click buy

  1. Know the number. Check the price history on the product page first. If it’s not below what it’s sold for before, it isn’t a deal.
  2. Check it’s Australian stock. Grey imports arrive without a local warranty, and espresso machines need servicing.
  3. Check who’s selling it. Marketplace sellers on any site are not the site itself. Returns and warranty get complicated.
  4. Check the model number, not the name. Manufacturers run near-identical variants with different bundles — a kit missing the grinder, a colour with no stock, last year’s model under this year’s name.
  5. Don’t buy a machine for the person you hope to become. Most dual boilers in Australian kitchens make two flat whites a day. A simpler machine, a good grinder and better beans beats an intimidating machine you stop using in March.
